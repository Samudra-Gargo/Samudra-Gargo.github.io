Our team builds an ultra-efficient car for Shell Eco-Marathon. I lead the electrical system, including the motor controller that turns the 48 V battery into three-phase drive for the brushless hub motor.

73.4 → 99.4 mi/kWhVehicle efficiency between the 2024 and 2025 competitions, after reworking MOSFET selection, gate drive and the power PCB.
  • Designed the inverter: STM32 PWM, a discrete MOSFET power stage and gate-driver circuitry, plus the embedded firmware that runs it.
  • Laid out the high-current power PCBs in Altium, with attention to MOSFET routing, DC-link capacitance, grounding and gate-driver placement to reduce switching transients.
  • Found shoot-through on the oscilloscope and tuned PWM timing and dead time until switching was clean.
  • Drew brushed and brushless controller schematics and full electrical schematics for two vehicles: battery, contactor, controller, hub motor and throttle.
  • Now: running control tasks on an RTOS and adding Hall-effect current sensing for real-time current monitoring.

PythonLinuxMAVSDKUART

Voice-controlled autonomous drone

Say "take off, then move forward five meters and turn left," and the drone does it. Everything runs onboard a Jetson Nano that talks to a Pixhawk 6C flight controller.

Bluetooth mic→Vosk speech recognition→Command parser→Command queue→MAVSDK→Pixhawk 6C (UART, 57600 baud)
  • Speech recognition on 16 kHz mono audio in 100 ms frames, with a parser that pulls distances, angles and units out of chained commands ("then", "and", "next").
  • Flight control through MAVSDK Offboard mode, with checks on armed, airborne and GPS state before every command.
  • Async, millisecond-timestamped logging so the command loop never blocks.
  • Configured the Jetson's UART for the flight controller (serial console off, udev permissions). Diagnosed propulsion faults, replaced an ESC and repaired the motors.
